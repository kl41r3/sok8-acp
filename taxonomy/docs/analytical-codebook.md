# Eight-function analytical codebook — corpus/taxonomy preparation

This table carries forward the author/user-defined analytical lens already used in the 2 October 2026 protocol draft. It is a codebook for comparing mechanism claims, not a released per-record taxonomy annotation. The preserved retrieval and screening freezes establish eligibility rules; they do not establish that these eight labels were preregistered before initial retrieval. No empirical eight-function classification of the 1,301 selected identifiers, saturation test, or coding agreement has been completed.

| Function | Established label | Operational comparison question |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Identity and Trust | What is named or authenticated, what does an issuer assert, what behavior does reputation describe, and what trust judgment follows from that evidence? |
| L2 | Discovery and Capability Representation | How are resources/capabilities described, published, indexed and matched, and what is established beyond relevance? |
| L3 | Orchestration and Delegation | What messages, tasks and delegated roles compose an interaction, and which responsibilities remain beyond interoperability? |
| L4 | Authorization/Mandates/Bounded Authority | What authority and constraints are represented, attenuated and checked, and how do those bounds relate to the principal's objective? |
| L5 | Payment and Settlement | What verifies authorization, commits payment or establishes finality, and what binds those states to delivery? |
| L6 | Commerce Lifecycle | How do checkout, ordering, fulfillment and post-purchase states transition, and what evidence supports reported outcomes? |
| L7 | Presentation/Interaction/Observability/Intervention | What can the user understand, observe, supply, approve, escalate or cancel, and before which irreversible action? |
| L8 | Disputes/Remedy/Enforcement | What dispute evidence exists, who adjudicates, what remedy is specified, and who or what executes it? |

A mechanism may address several functions. Naming/authentication/credentials/reputation are distinct; relevance is not trust; authorization is not objective satisfaction; settlement is not delivery; validation evidence is not automatically adjudication or enforcement. The functions are not a necessary stack, a completeness theorem, a reliability ranking, or measured corpus frequencies.
